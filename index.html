What's in the beta

Outreach

Two tracks. Email from a separate sending domain for offices, corporate and apartments. Social DMs only for events, venues and hospitality. Three touches each, in the chef's voice, tracked by TAZ.

Bookings + Social

A five-stage board from New to Booked, plus a weekly Instagram plan so the page stays warm while outreach runs.

The backend: how outreach runs

Every week the loop below runs. TAZ does the finding, writing and tracking. The chef does the tasting, the cooking and the closing conversation.

1FindPull Charlotte businesses by segment and drive ring: apartment communities, singles-event hosts, venues without kitchens, breweries and bars, planners. Sources: Google Business, LinkedIn, Instagram, Eventbrite and Meetup listings.
2ScoreFit (does this place buy group food?) plus timing (hiring, new location, event season). Only 80+ scores go to outreach.
3Reach3 touches over 6 days on the right channel: email from a Smartlead domain for offices, corporate and apartments; social DMs for events and hospitality. Every message names the business and the occasion.
4TasteThe offer is a free sample tray, not a discount. Tastings convert far better than price talk.
5BookQuote, deposit, prep day on the calendar. TAZ chases quotes older than 3 days.
6RepeatEvery booked client gets a reorder nudge before their next cycle and a 2-referral ask. Repeat and referral is where margin lives.

Target markets: go after these

Best fit

Apartment complexes (Uptown, South End, Ballantyne, University City)

  • Charlotte keeps adding towers and every one needs resident events to fill and keep units.
  • Community and lifestyle managers have a monthly events budget.
  • One management company can mean several buildings.
Best fit

Singles events and social hosts

  • Mixers, speed dating, supper clubs and run-club socials happen every week.
  • Organizers sell out and want a food partner; they live on Instagram, so a DM works.
  • A great tray at one event gets seen by 80 people who then follow the page.
Multiplier

Event venues with no kitchen

  • Lofts, warehouses, galleries and rooftops keep an approved-caterer list.
  • Get on the list and the venue sends you bookings.
  • Sample tray plus a one-page menu is the pitch.
Partnership

Breweries, taprooms and bars

  • Most have no kitchen and lean on food trucks.
  • A sushi pairing night is easy for them to promote.
  • Recurring weekly slots turn into steady revenue.
Multiplier

Event planners and venue coordinators

  • Each planner books many events a year; win the planner, not the event.
  • They value reliability and a distinctive menu over the lowest price.
Seasonal

Holiday and Q4 parties

  • Budgets open in October; outreach starts in September.
  • Higher ticket, same kitchen.
  • Use the booked list to pre-sell next year's dates.

Markets to avoid (for now)

Avoid

Hospitals, schools and government cafeterias

Procurement cycles, vendor onboarding, insurance and HACCP paperwork. Months of effort for thin margins. Revisit after a year of steady books.

Avoid

Hotel and convention catering contracts

Exclusive in-house catering, volume requirements and net-60 terms. A one-chef kitchen cannot carry the cash gap.

Avoid

Wedding marketplaces and lead-fee sites

Price shoppers, pay-per-lead fees, one-time buyers. Take weddings only by referral from planners already on the board.

Avoid

Anything beyond the 40 minute ring

Drive time eats margin and quality (sushi does not travel well). Rock Hill, Salisbury and Hickory wait until a second prep location exists.

Avoid

Wholesale and grocery

Different business: labeling, shelf life, slotting fees. It dilutes the catering brand and the chef's time.

Avoid

Big-company RFPs

Long forms, legal review, and they want a vendor with a fleet. Land their individual teams through office managers instead.

Six-week gameplan

Week 1

Confirm real menu, prices, prep days and the pickup address. Swap sample content for real. Turn on order payments.

Week 2

Load 200 Charlotte targets into Lead Radar across apartments, singles events, venues, breweries and planners. Approve the 3-touch sequence in the chef's voice.

Week 3

Outreach live: 40 targets a week. Instagram plan posting on schedule. First sample trays go out.

Week 4

Review replies and tastings. Cut the segment that is not answering, double the one that is.

Week 5

First reorder nudges and referral asks. Run a first resident event at one apartment community and a first food night at one brewery or singles event.

Week 6

Read the numbers against the targets above. Set Q4 party outreach. Decide on the second delivery ring.
